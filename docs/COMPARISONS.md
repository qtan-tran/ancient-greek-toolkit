# Comparing resources for learning Ancient Greek (v1)

> **Policy:** these tables only **evaluate and link to** resources; the project hosts no copyrighted content. Public-domain (PD) material may be quoted freely. All recommendations are written from the perspective of **self-taught learners working in a second language** (not English natives) — the project's own course exists precisely because of that gap.

## 1. Reference grammars

| Resource | Language | Cost / legal | Assessment for self-taught learners |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Smyth, *A Greek Grammar for Colleges* (1920)** | English | Free, PD — [electronic edition](https://grammars.alpheios.net/smyth/xhtml/smyth.html) · [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Smyth+Grammar+1a) · [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/smyth-greek-grammar-for-colleges-1920) | **The project's primary recommendation.** It is the "statute book" of Greek grammar: ~1500 numbered sections, indexed by subject, with examples from real texts. Inherent limitation: written for people who already know Greek — use it as a grammar *dictionary*, not as a course (don't read it cover to cover). |
| **Modern research grammars** (Schwyzer–Debrunner; Kühner–Gerth; Rijksbaron on the verb) | German / English | Paid / library — policy: link-only, no hosting | The scholarly standards, but far too heavy for the beginner stage. Only needed when reading research literature or settling a technical grammar dispute — borrow through a library then. |

**Recommendation:** Smyth (free, PD) as your primary reference + the Graecum VN lessons as your roadmap. When a grammar point exceeds the scope of a lesson, look the subject up in Smyth's index (e.g. "Third Declension", "Imperative", "Prohibitive").

## 2. Introductory textbooks (link-only; no detailed content review)

| Group | Typical examples (English) | The project's perspective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intensive | Hansen & Quinn; JACT *Reading Greek*; Mastronarde | Rich exercise sets, true classroom standards — but all in English, so learners whose first language is not English still learn "through a middleman language". Use them as supplementary exercise sources once academic English is comfortable. |
| Self-study | Hoberman; teach-yourself style books | Lighter, less systematic. Best treated as side reading, not as a spine. |
| For speakers of Vietnamese | (essentially none) | This is exactly the gap Graecum VN fills — the project's 48-lesson course is written for this audience, so its lessons replace, rather than point to, a textbook in this group. |
| For speakers of German | Kantharos-type Graecum courses | Structurally equivalent to Graecum I–II and very systematic — but it imposes a double language barrier (Ancient Greek through German). Only sensible above roughly B2 German. |

**Legal note:** the project deliberately **does not review the contents** of copyrighted textbooks in detail (avoiding both boilerplate reviews and any hint of its own editorial sources); the table above only locates each group of resources so you can decide what to borrow or buy.

## 3. Lexica

| Lexicon | Language | Cost / legal | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LSJ** (Liddell–Scott–Jones) | Greek → English | Free via [Logeion](https://logeion.uchicago.edu); PD editions exist | **The absolute standard** — every grammar dispute ends with "check LSJ". Heavy for beginners: start with Middle Liddell (same portal, much more compact). |
| Middle Liddell | Greek → English | Free via Logeion | The abridged LSJ for learners — **the right starting point** for the beginner stage. |
| Pape (19th century) | Greek → German | Free via Logeion, PD | Only useful if you read German; low priority for most learners. |
| Wiktionary | Greek → English (+ multilingual) | Free | Quick lookups; cross-check against LSJ because entries are community-written. |
| Greek → Vietnamese | — | — | **No full Ancient Greek–Vietnamese dictionary exists.** The project fills this gap piece by piece through the four-column vocabulary tables (Greek | transliteration | English | Vietnamese) in each lesson. |

## 4. A "minimal kit" strategy per level

- **Stage 1 (Graecum VN lessons 1–12):** the lessons + Anki for the ★ vocabulary. Reference: Smyth only when puzzled. Lexicon: Middle Liddell via Logeion.
- **Stage 2 (lessons 13–24):** add reading real texts on the Scaife Viewer (click each word) — reading-with-tools is a skill of its own that needs deliberate practice. Lexicon: LSJ gradually replaces Middle Liddell.
- **Stage 3 (Graecum II, lessons 25–48):** Smyth consulted reflexively; research-grade grammar (e.g. Rijksbaron on the verb) when needed; reading real texts becomes the main activity.
