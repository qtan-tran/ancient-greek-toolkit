Ancient Greek Toolkit

"grill me with ancient greek" — a curated hub of tools for learners of Ancient Greek: lexica & morphology, primary-text libraries, typing & fonts, practice, and reference grammar. Every tool listed here is free or public domain; every link was verified at the time of writing.

This toolkit accompanies the Graecum VN lesson portal (a complete 48-lesson course, currently published in Vietnamese with an English edition under way): the lessons are the roadmap, this toolkit is the gear. Looking for guidance on which grammar, textbook, or lexicon to add to your shelf? See the resource comparison tables.

1. Lexica & morphology lookup

The most-used category: you meet an unknown word, you look it up. Start from Logeion (LSJ is the standard lexicon); Wiktionary is a quick first pass, but cross-check against LSJ.

ToolWhat it isUse it when…Link
Logeion freeA one-stop lookup portal: the LSJ, Middle Liddell, Pape, Slater lexica plus automatic morphology analysis for the word you look up.You need a word's full lexicographic entry (LSJ), or want several lexica side by side.logeion.uchicago.edu
Wiktionary — Ancient Greek freeQuick glosses and declension tables edited by the community; good for getting the basic sense of a word.You want a fast first answer; cross-check against LSJ via Logeion, since entries are community-written.Wiktionary: Ancient Greek
Diogenes freeOpen-source desktop application that reads the LSJ and Slater lexica offline; works without a network connection (you supply the dictionary data files yourself).You want offline LSJ lookup at native speed, with Greek-text support.github.com/pjheslin/diogenes

2. Primary-text libraries

Reading real texts is the point of every lesson. The Scaife Viewer is the friendliest experience for beginners (click any word for its morphology); the Perseus Hopper is the longest-standing library; Theoi is strongest on mythology.

ToolWhat it isUse it when…Link
Perseus Digital Library (Tufts) freeThe oldest open digital library of Ancient Greek texts with English translations; includes a word study tool that parses each word.You want to read a text with translation and cite a passage by author and work.perseus.tufts.edu
Scaife Viewer freeThe modern interface to the Perseus 5.0 corpus: click a word to see its morphology, read a facing translation, and build vocabulary lists per passage.You want the smoothest reading experience with in-text morphology (recommended for beginners).scaife.perseus.org
Theoi Classical Texts Library freeA library of Greek originals with English translations, organized by mythological figure and by author; public-domain sources.You are reading mythological texts or hunting a passage by character/author.theoi.com/Library
Greek Wikisource freeCommunity-proofed transcriptions of Greek texts (ancient and modern), interface in Modern Greek.You need a transcription when Perseus/Scaife do not have the work.el.wikisource.org

3. Typing Greek & fonts

Typing polytonic Greek (breathings and accents) is a skill you need early: notes, flashcards, lookup. Three tools in order of commitment: TypeGreek (nothing to install) → Keyman (a real keyboard layout) → the Gentium font for beautiful display.

ToolWhat it isUse it when…Link
TypeGreek freeType polytonic Greek on a Latin keyboard: type the letters, then press the accent/breathing keys to combine.You need to type a few words or sentences on a machine without a Greek keyboard.typegreek.com
Keyman — Greek keyboards freeGreek keyboard layouts (including fully polytonic ones) for Windows, macOS, Linux and mobile.You type Greek regularly, in any application. Tip: Windows and macOS both ship a built-in "Greek Polytonic" keyboard in the system language settings.keyman.com/keyboards
Gentium Plus (SIL) freeA free font with full polytonic Greek support (accents, breathings, iota subscript) that displays beautifully in lessons and texts.Greek renders poorly or without diacritics on your machine → install this font and set it as the default for Greek text.software.sil.org/gentium

5. Reference grammar & resource comparisons

The lessons teach in sequence; when you need to look a grammar point up in depth, use Smyth — the standard reference grammar, public domain, ~1500 numbered sections. Three independent electronic copies (if one URL dies, the other two keep working — the project's sustainability rule).

Smyth, A Greek Grammar for Colleges (1920) public domainThe standard reference grammar of Ancient Greek (Herbert Weir Smyth), in an easy-to-navigate electronic edition.You need to research a grammar point beyond the scope of the lessons — use the index of subjects.grammars.alpheios.net
