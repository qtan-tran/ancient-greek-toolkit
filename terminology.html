Terminology

The standard English terms for Ancient Greek grammar — the search keys that unlock LSJ, Smyth, Scaife and every scholarly tool. Two companion pages: this one (English, global audience) and the Vietnamese terminology page of Graecum VN, which maps each English term to the Vietnamese term used in the Vietnamese lessons.

Why terms matter: tools answer only well-formed queries. If you know the word is "third declension", Smyth's index opens the right section; if all you have is "weird nouns ending in -ος", nothing opens. This page gives every standard term a one-line definition plus a Vietnamese cross-reference.

1. Writing system & sounds

English termDefinitionGraecum VN (Vietnamese)
polytonic GreekThe classical writing system with full accent marks; distinct from the monotonic system of Modern Greek.chữ đa thanh
accent (acute ´, grave `, circumflex ~)A written pitch mark over a vowel. An oxytone word carries it on the last syllable; the grave is merely the written variant of the acute before another word.dấu thanh (sắc / huyền / tròn); oxytone
rough breathing (spiritus asper)The small ὁ-shaped mark over an initial vowel: adds the sound /h/. Smooth breathing (spiritus lenis) marks an initial vowel with no /h/.dấu khó thở / dấu hơi thở
iota subscriptA small ι written under a long vowel (τῷ, ᾧ); grammatically important in the dative.iota phụ
elisionTwo vowels colliding: the first withdraws and is written as an apostrophe (ἀλλ' ἐγώ). Latin elides in pronunciation; Greek writes it into the orthography.né vực nguyên âm / lược âm
movable nuAn ν optionally added at a word end before a vowel (ἐστίν / ἐστι) — orthography, not meaning.ν di động
enclitic / procliticA word leaning on the following (ὁ, ἡ) or preceding (μου, σου, τι) word for its accent, carrying no accent mark of its own.enclitic / proclitic (từ "núp" / "dính")

2. Nouns, adjectives & the article

English termDefinitionGraecum VN (Vietnamese)
case (Nom., Gen., Dat., Acc., Voc.)The role-tag of a noun: nominative (subject), genitive ("of"), dative ("to/with/in"), accusative (direct object), vocative (addressing).cách: chủ cách / sở hữu cách / tặng cách / đối cách / hô cách
declensionA class of nouns sharing one set of endings. Greek: o-declension and the third declension (consonant stems). Latin: 1st / 2nd / 3rd.hệ thống chia; nhóm o; chia thứ ba
stem / endingThe meaning-bearing part of the word + the role-tag ending attached to it. In the third declension the stem is hidden in the nominative and revealed by the genitive.thân / đuôi; thân ẩn, thân trần
articleὁ, ἡ, τό — Greek has one, Latin has none. This is the single biggest structural difference between the two languages.mạo từ
gender (m./f./n.)Masculine, feminine, neuter. Neuters: nominative = accusative; plural subject takes a singular verb.giống đực / cái / trung
number (sg./pl., dual)Singular and plural — plus the Greek dual ("the two"), which Latin lacks.số ít / số nhiều; số kép
attributive positionThe slot arrangement that marks an adjective as describing the noun (article + adjective + noun). Outside it, the adjective is a predicate.vị trí định ngữ; định ngữ / biểu ngữ
substantivized adjectiveArticle + adjective used as a noun: τὸ ἀγαθόν "the good (thing)", ὁ ἐχθρός "the enemy".tính từ danh từ hóa
ellipsisOmitting words already clear from context; its result is the nominal sentence (no verb).lược từ; câu danh ngữ
demonstrative pronounὅδε, ἐκεῖνος "this / that": Greek has no separate this/that-words; pronoun + article do the work (deixis: near / just-mentioned / far).đại từ chỉ định; hướng chỉ

3. Verbs

English termDefinitionGraecum VN (Vietnamese)
person (1st/2nd/3rd)1/2/3 × sg./pl. = six forms; the subject usually hides inside the ending.ngôi
tenseGreek tenses bundle time with aspect (how the action is viewed: ongoing / completed / simple). Present = the first tense learned.thời
voice (active / middle / passive)Greek has a true middle voice (the subject acts on/for itself); in the present, middle and passive share one set of forms (middle/passive).thức: chủ động / trung / bị động
mood (indicative, imperative, subjunctive, optative)The speaker's stance: stating, commanding, wishing. Greek keeps a distinct optative; Latin merged it into the subjunctive.cách thức: chỉ định, mệnh lệnh (mong ước, giả định)
imperative, incl. 3rd-person imperativeCommand forms for 2nd AND 3rd person: λυέτω / λυέτωσαν "let him/them release". Latin has no form of its own and borrows the subjunctive (amet).mệnh lệnh cách; mệnh lệnh ngôi 3
prohibitiveμὴ + imperative = "don't!" — distinct from factual negation οὐ. Latin: nē.câu cấm đoán; phủ định οὐ / μή
infinitiveThe "to do" form: subject with δεῖ, object in the AcI construction.nguyên mẫu
tense stemThe stem a given tense is built from: stem + thematic vowel + ending.thân thời
thematic / athematicVerbs with the connecting vowel thematic vowel -ο-/ε- vs verbs without it (δύναμαι, εἶναι). The distinction reshuffles every paradigm.có chủ đề / không chủ đề; nguyên âm chủ đề
deponent verbMiddle/passive in form, active in meaning: μάχομαι, δύναμαι. Latin: sequor, loquor.động từ deponent

4. Syntax

English termDefinitionGraecum VN (Vietnamese)
subject / objectThe doer vs the undergoer; Greek usually leaves both unexpressed when the verb ending carries them.chủ ngữ / tân ngữ
double accusativeOne verb governing two accusatives: person + thing (διδάσκω) or object + predicate (νομίζω).đối cách kép
accusative + infinitive (AcI)"Someone to do something" after verbs of thinking/saying: νομίζω αὐτὸν σοφὸν εἶναι. Latin has the same construction (puto Socratem sapientem esse).nguyên mẫu tân ngữ (AcI)
dative of possession / instrumental dative"At someone there is…" / "by means of…". Latin prefers the ablative for both jobs.tặng cách sở hữu / tặng cách công cụ
two-way prepositionμετά / πρός: with accusative = motion towards; with dative = being with. Latin splits the same work between prepositions and the ablative.giới từ lưỡng tính

5. Greek vs Latin: what each language has that the other lacks

Both are close relatives in the Indo-European family, and most grammar concepts transfer. But each has "specialties" a bilingual learner must route around:

Greek onlyLatin only
Writingrough/smooth breathings; iota subscript; written accents (acute/grave/circumflex)length marked only as ā/ē in dictionaries — no written accents
Nounsdefinite article (ὁ, ἡ, τό); dual numberablative case (Greek uses dative + genitive for its jobs); gerund / gerundive; supine
Verbstrue middle voice; distinct optative mood; a dedicated 3rd-person imperative (-έτω)simple future markers (-b-); 3rd-person commands borrowed from the subjunctive (amet)
Sharedfive core cases (Nom./Gen./Dat./Acc./Voc.; Latin adds Abl. → six); declensions; deponent verbs; AcI; participles; elision; enclitics; the shared Indo-European inheritance (sum/εἰμί, ego/ἐγώ)
